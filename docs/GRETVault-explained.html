Chapter 1

The big picture

GRET is a share of a basket of tokenized assets. One contract issues the shares and holds the basket, and it enforces every rule on its own.

1.1One contract, one token

GRETVault is two things at once. It is the GRET token, a standard ERC-20 that wallets such as MetaMask can show and that can be transferred freely. And it is the vault that holds the basket: today 11 tokens, from ITOTon and SPYx to PAXG and cbBTC. The token's address is the vault's address.

Holding GRET means owning a proportional part of everything in the vault. If 100 GRET exist, each one represents 1% of every token the vault holds. Minting and redeeming use no prices and no stored value per GRET: the contract only counts tokens. Prices appear in one place only, to limit what a swap can lose (chapter 7).

1.4Three ideas to keep in mind

  1. The rules live in the vault. The contract's code cannot change after deployment. Governance decides what to do, but always inside limits the vault enforces: waits, caps and what can never be touched.
  2. Nobody takes reserves for free. Tokens leave the vault only when someone burns GRET (a redemption or a liquidation claim) or when the rebalancer delivers another basket token in exchange.
  3. Big changes are announced. A new token, a new rebalancer, a new governance, a write-off or a fee raise waits 7 days, visible on the blockchain, and then must be carried out within the next 7 days or it expires.

Chapter 2

Minting GRET

To get new GRET you deposit a little of every token in the basket, in the same proportions the vault already holds. Anyone can mint.

2.1How much you deposit

The amount of each token behind 1 GRET is that token's reserve divided by the GRET in circulation. This number is not stored anywhere: the vault computes it on every operation from what it actually holds. To mint, you deposit exactly that proportion of every token.

Example

The vault holds 10 PAXG and 100 GRET are in circulation, so each GRET stands for 0.1 PAXG. To mint 1 GRET you deposit 0.1 PAXG, plus the matching part of each other token. If the vault also holds 0.5 cbBTC, you deposit 0.005 cbBTC too.

A token with no reserve (just added, sold off, lost or written off) is not asked for at all. previewMint(shares) tells you the exact amounts before you mint.

2.2The first mint

When no GRET is in circulation there are no proportions to copy, so the first mint uses the initial basket, a fixed list of amounts. It must ask for exactly 1 GRET and it issues exactly 1 GRET. In the pilot, the initial basket is worth about USD 100. The same happens again if every holder leaves and the supply returns to zero. Governance can change the initial basket with setInitialAmounts, which only matters at zero supply.

2.3Step by step

  1. You approve the vault to take each token, then call mint(shares, maxAmountsIn, minShares, version).
  2. The vault first records any loss (if it holds less of a token than it recorded, see chapter 5), so you never pay for someone else's loss.
  3. It computes what to ask for, rounding up, and pulls each token from your wallet.
  4. It measures what actually arrived and issues GRET for that, rounding down.
  5. If there is a mint fee, part of the new GRET goes to the fee recipient (chapter 4). The rest goes to you.
  6. Your wallet is marked as having minted, which allows it to redeem from then on.
Example: a mint with a new token and an xStock

The vault holds 10 PAXG, 100 SPYx and no cbBTC yet (it was just added), and 100 GRET are in circulation. You ask for 1 GRET.

  1. The vault asks for 0.1 PAXG and 1 SPYx, and nothing of cbBTC, which has no reserve.
  2. PAXG arrives exactly. SPYx is an xStock and arrives 2 base units short: 0.999999999999999998 SPYx.
  3. For each token, the vault works out how much GRET what arrived is worth, and issues the smallest: 0.999999999999999998 GRET, that is, 1 GRET minus 2 wei. You absorb the 2 missing units; nobody else does.

2.4Protections and limits

  • Rounding favors everyone else. You pay a hair more and receive a hair less, so minting never lowers the value behind other holders' GRET.
  • You set your own limits. maxAmountsIn caps what you pay for each token, and minShares is the least GRET you accept after the fee. If either is crossed, nothing happens.
  • Your limits stay on the right tokens. maxAmountsIn follows the order of components(), and version is the basketVersion() you read with it. If a token is added or removed before your transaction lands, the version no longer matches and the mint reverts, instead of applying your limits to another token.
  • Tokens that round. xStocks (SPYx, JAAAx) lose 2 or 3 base units per transfer. The vault tolerates up to 10 base units short and simply issues GRET for what arrived. A bigger shortfall, such as a transfer fee, makes the mint revert.
  • When minting is closed. While governance has paused it (section 6.8), during a liquidation, and if every reserve has been lost (the vault refuses to issue GRET backed by nothing).

Chapter 3

Redeeming GRET

Redeeming is minting in reverse: you hand in GRET and receive your proportional part of every token in the basket.

3.1Who can redeem

Only wallets that have minted at least once can redeem normally. Once a wallet has minted, it can also redeem GRET it bought or received later. A wallet that only ever bought GRET on the market has to mint once first, which it cannot do while governance has minting paused (section 6.8) or while a frozen token still has a reserve, since that mint must deposit it. This rule does not apply in a liquidation, where every holder can exit (chapter 8).

3.2How much you receive

Call redeem(shares, minAmountsOut, version). If there is a redemption fee, its part of your GRET goes to the fee recipient and only the rest is burned. For each token you receive burned GRET ÷ GRET in circulation × that token's reserve, rounded down. minAmountsOut sets the least you accept of each token, and previewRedeem(shares) shows the amounts in advance, after the fee. As in a mint, version ties your minimums to the basket you saw.

Example

The vault holds 10 PAXG and 4 cbBTC, and 100 GRET are in circulation. You redeem 5 GRET with no fee: you receive 5% of each reserve, 0.5 PAXG and 0.2 cbBTC, and the supply drops to 95 GRET.

3.3The 1 GRET floor

A redemption cannot leave between 0 and 1 GRET in circulation: either everyone leaves, or at least 1 GRET remains. Without this floor, tiny leftovers could distort the basket and make minting unusable. A consequence: if someone keeps a tiny amount of GRET, the last large holder cannot redeem their final bit normally; it can be recovered through liquidation or by gathering the GRET in one wallet.

3.4All or nothing, and the emergency exit

A normal redemption sends every token in one operation. If a single token cannot be sent (its issuer froze the vault, for example), the whole redemption reverts and you keep your GRET.

That is why there is a second way out: redeemSkipping(shares, minAmountsOut, skipped, version). It is the same redemption, but it leaves out the tokens you list. The vault does not even call them, so no token can block it, however it misbehaves. You give up your part of the skipped tokens: it stays in the vault for the other holders (or, if you were the last holder, it becomes surplus). Set the minimum of a skipped token to 0.

Example: leaving while a token is frozen

The vault holds 10 PAXG, 4 cbBTC and 100 SPYx, and 100 GRET are in circulation. SPYx's issuer freezes the vault, so a plain redemption reverts. You hold 10 GRET and call redeemSkipping listing SPYx:

  • you receive 10% of the PAXG and cbBTC: 1 PAXG and 0.4 cbBTC;
  • your 10 SPYx stay in the vault, so the remaining 90 GRET now share 100 SPYx, about 1.11 per GRET instead of 1.

If SPYx recovers, those who stayed keep your part. That is the price of leaving early.

Why the emergency exit matters

Your protection against a malicious rebalancer is the 7-day notice: you can leave before it takes over. Without redeemSkipping, a captured governance could make one token fail on purpose and trap everyone during those 7 days. With it, whoever minted can always leave with every token that still works. Holders who never minted are not covered: governance can pause minting so that they cannot qualify (section 6.8).

Chapter 4

Fees

Governance can charge a fee on mints and on redemptions. Both start at zero, are paid in GRET and can never exceed 5%.

4.1The mint fee

You deposit the basket for all the GRET the mint issues, and the fee's share of that GRET goes to the fee recipient. With a 1% fee, minting 1 GRET gives you 0.99 GRET and the recipient 0.01. The minter pays the whole fee, so the value behind every other GRET does not change.

The mint fee also protects holders. Someone who already holds the basket could mint just before a sync and redeem just after, taking part of a distribution that belongs to others. A quarterly dividend is about 0.3% of its token, which is only part of the basket, so a mint fee around 0.1% makes that unprofitable. The agreed policy is to set one once third parties can mint.

4.2The redemption fee

The fee's share of the GRET you hand in goes to the recipient, and only the rest is burned and paid out in tokens. With a 2% fee, redeeming 1 GRET moves 0.02 GRET to the recipient and pays you the tokens for 0.98 GRET.

4.3Limits and changes

  • Cap: at most 5% each, written in the code.
  • Raising waits: a raise is announced and can take effect only after 7 days, during the following 7 days; anyone can apply it then. A cut applies at once and cancels any pending raise.
  • Recipient: chosen by governance, at once. Once set, it can never be empty again, so a fee always has somewhere to go.
  • Never charged on liquidation exits, swaps or ordinary GRET transfers.

4.4Side effects

  • The recipient's GRET is ordinary GRET and stays in circulation. If the recipient holds less than 1 GRET, it counts against the 1 GRET floor, so the last holders cannot fully redeem until the recipient hands its GRET over. In the pilot the recipient would be the pilot wallet.
  • The Minted and Redeemed events report net amounts; the fee appears in its own FeeCharged event.

Chapter 5

The basket and its reserves

The vault keeps its own record of what backs GRET, separate from whatever happens to sit in its balance. That separation is what keeps everyone's share honest.

5.1Components

The basket's tokens are called components. There can be between 1 and 128. The pilot starts with 11: ITOTon, SPYx, EFAon, IEMGon, SGOVon, BILon, JAAAx, PAXG, cbBTC, VNQon and DBCon. The cap of 128 exists because mint and redemption visit every component in one transaction, and Ethereum limits the gas a single transaction can use (about 16.7M). With 128 tokens like today's, a mint would take about 8.7M.

5.2Reserve, balance and surplus

TermMeaning
ReserveWhat the vault recognizes as backing GRET. It changes only through mints, redemptions, swaps, recognized losses, write-offs and sync.
BalanceWhat the vault actually holds of a token, as the token contract reports it.
SurplusBalance above the reserve: donations, issuer distributions, mistaken transfers. It does not back GRET until governance decides.

5.3Losses

If the vault holds less of a token than its reserve (an issuer seizure, a negative rebase, an issuer fee), the reserve drops to the real balance and the loss is shared by every holder in proportion. Mint, redemption and swap do this on their own, and anyone can trigger it with recognizeLoss.

5.4Surpluses: sync and rescue

A surplus is not added on its own. Governance decides, case by case:

  • sync(token) adds the surplus to the reserve, for the benefit of every holder. The agreed policy is to sync issuer distributions, which belong to the holders, as soon as they arrive, pausing minting around the sync. The pause only stops whoever arrives after it: someone who minted before, having seen the surplus, still takes a share. Once third parties can mint, a small mint fee makes that unprofitable (section 4.1).
  • rescue(token, to, amount) withdraws it: for a component, only what exceeds what is owed to holders; for a token outside the basket, all of it. Never a written-off token.

If someone sends GRET itself to the vault's address by mistake, the transfer simply fails, so nothing is lost and governance never holds GRET.

Chapter 6

Governance

Governance is a single address that decides changes. Today it is the pilot wallet; later it can be a multisig, a timelock or a vote. Whatever it is, the vault sets the limits.

6.1What governance can and cannot do

It can
  • Add, remove and write off components
  • Set the initial basket
  • Set fees within 5%
  • Appoint and revoke the rebalancer
  • Sync or rescue surpluses
  • Pause and resume minting
  • Start a liquidation
  • Appoint its successor
It cannot
  • Withdraw reserves or liquidation rights
  • Mint GRET
  • Change the contract's code
  • Pause redemptions or reverse a liquidation
  • Skip any of the waits
  • Swap, unless it appoints itself rebalancer

The rules must live in the vault, not in the governance contract, because governance can be replaced. If the 7-day wait lived in governance, a new or captured governance could arrive without it. Think of the vault as a safe with a time lock: the board decides to open it, but the lock belongs to the safe, and it stays when the board changes.

6.2How a change works: wait, then a window

This applies to adding a token, writing one off, raising fees, appointing a rebalancer and handing over governance. Three of them (adding, writing off and applying a fee raise) can be carried out by anyone once ready: governance already decided when it proposed, so a voting governance needs only one vote per change. The window keeps an old notice from being used by surprise months later.

6.3Adding a token

  1. Governance announces it with announceComponent(token). It must be a contract and not already in the basket.
  2. From day 7 to day 14, anyone calls addComponent(token). It joins at the end of the list, with zero reserve and zero initial amount.
  3. Nothing changes for holders yet: mint does not ask for a token with no reserve. It starts counting once the rebalancer buys some with a swap (chapter 7).

Governance can cancel with cancelComponentAnnouncement during the wait. Tokens cannot be added during a liquidation or beyond 128.

6.4Removing a token

  1. The rebalancer sells the whole reserve through swaps, until it is 0.
  2. Governance calls removeComponent(token). There is no wait, because a token with no reserve backs nothing.
  3. The tokens after it move down one position. Any leftover balance becomes a token outside the basket, which governance may rescue.
  4. Adding or removing a token raises basketVersion by one, so mints and redemptions prepared for the old list revert instead of applying their limits to the wrong token.

It cannot remove the last component, a written-off one, or anything during a liquidation. If a mint lands just before the final sale and leaves a remainder, a second small swap sells it.

6.5Writing off a broken token

If an issuer freezes the vault, minting stops, every redemption has to skip that token with redeemSkipping, and the token cannot be sold because the vault cannot send it. The write-off lets the vault work normally again without it.

  1. Governance proposes it with proposeWriteOff(token). It judges that the token is broken; the vault does not check.
  2. From day 7 to day 14, anyone calls writeOffComponent(token).
  3. The token's reserve and initial amount drop to 0. Mint and redemption continue without it.
Why it cannot be used to steal

The tokens stay in the vault, the written-off token stays in the basket for good, and rescue refuses it forever. If it recovers, or if it was written off by mistake, sync returns it to the holders at once, even during a liquidation. Holders lose that token's share only while it stays broken.

6.6Appointing the rebalancer

There is no rebalancer at deployment. Governance proposes one with proposeRebalancer(address), and that address accepts with acceptRebalancer between day 7 and day 14. Governance can revoke the rebalancer at once with revokeRebalancer, which also discards one still pending: taking power away never needs a wait.

Before any swap, governance must also appoint a price guard with proposePriceGuard(address). After the same 7 days anyone applies it with applyPriceGuard, until day 14. Section 7.4 explains what it does.

6.7Setting fees

Governance calls proposeFees(mintFee, redeemFee), in basis points (100 = 1%). Lower values apply at once. Higher ones wait and are applied with applyFees between day 7 and day 14. Governance chooses the recipient with setFeeRecipient. Details in chapter 4.

6.8Pausing the mint

Governance can pause minting, and resume it, at once with setMintPaused. Everyone who minted can still redeem, normally or with the emergency exit. A wallet that never minted, though, cannot qualify while the pause lasts: if it bought GRET, it has to sell it or wait. This is an accepted risk (R-07 in section 11.4): redemption stays for wallets that minted, and a pause that let new wallets mint would not serve its purpose.

The pause is useful during an incident, while something is investigated, and around a sync: pause, sync, resume, so nobody can mint between the pause and the sync. It does not stop someone who minted before the pause from sharing in the sync, so a surplus should be synced as soon as it appears.

There is no way to pause redemptions. That is deliberate: a pause on exits is exactly the power to trap holders during the 7-day notice of a new rebalancer. For real emergencies the tools are the write-off and the liquidation.

6.9Handing over governance

The current governance calls proposeGovernance(next), and the new one accepts with acceptGovernance between day 7 and day 14. A timelock with its own delay of up to 14 days can still accept in time if it schedules the acceptance right away.

6.10From a wallet to a vote

For the vault, governance is just one address, and it can change: from the pilot wallet to a multisig, then a timelock, and finally a vote by VORA holders. Each step uses the handover of section 6.9, without touching the vault. Chapter 10 explains every stage and how voting works.

Mind the names: the Governor's propose creates a vote; the vault's announceComponent starts a token's 7-day wait.

Chapter 7

The rebalancer and swaps

Rebalancing means changing what the vault holds. The rebalancer does it by exchanging one basket token for another, and mint and redemption follow on their own.

7.1Why nobody sets "how much to deposit"

It might seem simpler for governance to declare "from today, 1 GRET costs 0.5 PAXG". It cannot work. Mint and redemption must follow what the vault holds, or new minters would take value from existing holders, and without prices the vault cannot tell whether two different baskets are worth the same.

The problem in numbers

The vault holds 10 PAXG and 10 GRET: 1 PAXG per GRET. If someone could mint 1 GRET paying 0.5 PAXG, there would be 10.5 PAXG for 11 GRET, about 0.95 PAXG each. The newcomer paid 0.5 and holds 0.95; each earlier holder lost 0.05.

7.2What a swap does

The rebalancer calls swap(tokenOut, amountOut, tokenIn, amountIn). Both amounts are fixed in the call: together they are the price.

  1. The vault records any loss on both tokens.
  2. It collects amountIn of the incoming token from the rebalancer and measures what arrived, with the same tolerance as mint.
  3. Only then does it send amountOut of the outgoing token. Only reserve can leave, never surplus.
  4. Both reserves change; the GRET supply does not.
Example

100 GRET are in circulation and the vault holds 10 PAXG and 0.5 cbBTC, so 1 GRET = 0.1 PAXG + 0.005 cbBTC. The rebalancer delivers 0.05 cbBTC and takes 1 PAXG. Now the vault holds 9 PAXG and 0.55 cbBTC, and 1 GRET = 0.09 PAXG + 0.0055 cbBTC. The next mint asks for exactly that.

At October 22 prices (PAXG ≈ USD 4,050, cbBTC ≈ USD 81,150), 1 PAXG ≈ 0.05 cbBTC, so the price was fair. Had the rebalancer delivered only 0.01 cbBTC, holders would have lost the difference.

7.3Trust and its limits

The rebalancer chooses the price; the price guard bounds it. A malicious rebalancer could still sell a little cheaply or buy a worthless token that governance added, but only within the guard's limits. The defenses are the price guard, the 7-day notice before any new rebalancer or guard acts, and the emergency exit of chapter 3.

  • Pilot: the pilot wallet, with the Chainlink guard.
  • Later: a bot or contract that finds the best price, still within the guard. It would be appointed through the same 7-day wait, without changing the vault.

7.4The price guard

Every swap must be accepted by the price guard, a separate contract whose address the vault keeps. Governance appoints it like a rebalancer, with the 7-day wait, and without a guard there are no swaps. Mint, redemption and liquidation never ask it anything, so not even a broken guard can block an exit.

The first guard, ChainlinkPriceGuard, values both sides of each swap in dollars with Chainlink price feeds and refuses:

  • a token with no feed, or whose price is stale, such as a stock feed outside market hours;
  • a swap that loses more than 2% of what the vault sells;
  • a swap that would make the week's swaps take more than 2% of the vault's value. Each loss counts as the share of the vault it takes at that moment, which is what every GRET loses, so money that comes in or leaves during the week does not stretch the limit. The vault is valued by what it really holds, so tokens an issuer took away, and that nobody has recorded yet, do not count either.
Why a weekly limit

With only a per-swap limit, a stolen rebalancer key could trade the same value back and forth: USD 1,000 for 980, then 980 for 960, and so on. Fifty swaps, each within 2%, would leave 364. The weekly limit caps the total: in a week, all swaps together can take at most 2% of the vault, however many there are. An honest rebalance barely touches it: selling USD 300 at 0.5% below the price takes 0.15% of the vault.

The guard has no owner. Its feeds and limits are fixed when it is deployed, and changing any of them means deploying a new guard and waiting the 7 days. It is only as honest as its prices: a captured governance could appoint a guard that accepts everything, but the 7-day notice gives holders time to leave, as with a rebalancer. And it only sees what its feeds see: if a token trades far below the asset its feed prices, a swap that buys it looks fair and is not limited at all (R-03 in section 11.4).

Today Chainlink has feeds on Ethereum for five of the eleven tokens: SPYx (through SPY's price), SGOVon, BILon, PAXG and cbBTC. The other six cannot be swapped until feeds exist and a new guard is appointed. Stock feeds pause on weekends, so swaps of those tokens do too.

7.5Funding a swap

Because the vault collects the incoming token first, the rebalancer must have it before the swap. There are three ways to do that without changing the vault:

  • Own funds, in steps. Buy some cbBTC, swap it in, receive PAXG, sell the PAXG and repeat. You need the capital of one step, not of the whole rebalance. This is the pilot's way.
  • A flash loan. A small rebalancer contract borrows the incoming token (for example, cbBTC from Morpho or Aave), swaps it into the vault, sells the token it receives on an exchange and repays the loan, all in one transaction. It needs no capital, only exchange liquidity for both tokens. The contract chooses the price, so it should deliver what the exchange pays, minus costs.
  • An auction. Each bidder brings its own incoming token; the auction contract calls the swap and hands over what comes out. Bidders compete, which also gives better prices.

Swaps are closed during a liquidation. There is no cap on how much can be swapped, only on how much swaps can lose: a volume cap would slow down a stolen key, but also a legitimate large rebalance.

Chapter 8

Liquidation

Liquidation is the emergency exit for everyone. It ends normal operation for good and lets every holder take their share, token by token.

8.1Starting it

Only governance can start a liquidation, with activateLiquidation. It is irreversible. It closes mint, normal redemption, swaps and basket changes, and it freezes the reserves and the GRET supply at that moment as the basis of every holder's right. It does not call any token, so a broken token cannot prevent it.

The supply frozen at activation never changes afterwards. Each holder's right over a token is GRET converted ÷ supply at activation × that token's reserve, so converting early or late, all at once or in several steps, gives exactly the same share. What someone claims is recorded apart and never lowers the reserve the others' shares are computed on.

8.2Exiting token by token

  1. Any holder, whether they minted or not, calls convertToClaims(shares). Their GRET is burned and turned into rights over every token. This can be done in several steps, and unconverted GRET stays transferable.
  2. For each token, the holder calls claim(index, to, minAmount). Each token is claimed separately, and can be sent to another address if, for example, an issuer blocks the holder's own.

No fee is charged on any of this.

8.3When a token falls short or fails

  • A token fails to transfer: that claim reverts and the right stays pending, ready to claim later. The other tokens are unaffected.
  • The vault holds less than everyone is owed: each claimant is paid the same fraction. minAmount lets a holder refuse a cut and wait, in case the balance recovers.
  • Tokens arrive later: governance can sync them, which raises everyone's rights in proportion, or rescue only what exceeds what is owed.
Example: a liquidation with a shortfall

Governance starts a liquidation with 100 GRET in circulation and reserves of 10 PAXG and 100 SPYx. Alice holds 50 GRET, Bob 30 and Carol 20, and all three convert. Their rights: Alice 5 PAXG and 50 SPYx, Bob 3 PAXG and 30 SPYx, Carol 2 PAXG and 20 SPYx. Everyone claims their PAXG in full.

Then SPYx's issuer seizes 40 SPYx: the vault holds 60 but owes 100.

  1. Alice claims SPYx now. Everyone pending gets the same fraction, 60 ÷ 100, so she receives 30 SPYx, and her SPYx right is used up.
  2. Bob sets minAmount to 30 and waits: a cut makes his claim revert, and his right stays pending.
  3. The issuer returns the 40 SPYx. The vault holds 70 and owes 50 to Bob and Carol, so both are paid in full: 30 and 20.
  4. 20 SPYx are left over: exactly Alice's cut. The agreed policy is for governance to rescue them and return them to Alice.

Chapter 10

Governance in practice: from a wallet to a VORA vote

The vault only checks which address is governance. Who stands behind that address, and how they decide, can grow over time without changing the vault. This chapter walks through each stage, ending with on-chain voting by VORA holders using OpenZeppelin's standard contracts.

10.1Four stages

StageHow a decision is madeWhat it takes
1. Pilot walletOne signature, at once. The current stage.Nothing
2. SafeSeveral signers must approve, for example 2 of 3, at once.A Safe created at app.safe.global; no code
3. Safe + timelockThe Safe schedules each action, and it runs only after a public delay.OpenZeppelin's TimelockController, already tested with the vault
4. VORA vote + timelockVORA holders vote; what passes waits in the same timelock, then runs.OpenZeppelin's Governor, and VORA built on ERC20Votes

Each move from one stage to the next is a governance handover (section 6.9): the current governance proposes the next address, and that address accepts between day 7 and day 14.

10.2Stage 2: a multisig

A Safe is a wallet that needs several signatures to act: with 2 of 3, any two of three signers must approve every transaction. Losing one key no longer locks governance, and stealing one key is not enough to control it. A Safe can also bundle several calls into one transaction, which all succeed or all fail together.

To move there, the pilot wallet calls proposeGovernance(safe), and the Safe calls acceptGovernance between day 7 and day 14. Decisions are still immediate: the Safe acts as soon as enough signers approve.

10.3Stage 3: a timelock

A TimelockController is a contract that holds a queue of actions. Every action is scheduled first and can be executed only after a minimum delay, so anyone watching the chain sees it coming. The timelock becomes the vault's governance; the Safe keeps the power to schedule.

Timelock roleWhat it allowsProposed holder
ProposerSchedule actions (each proposer is also made a canceller)The Safe
ExecutorRun an action once its delay has passed; can be open to anyoneThe Safe
CancellerRemove a scheduled action before it runsThe Safe
AdminChange the rolesNobody: the timelock manages itself

The delays add up. With a 2-day timelock, adding a token takes 2 days for the timelock plus the vault's own 7 days: holders get 9 days of notice. Liquidation, which the vault does not delay, would then wait the timelock's 2 days.

One detail of the handover

The timelock must accept governance itself, which means the call acceptGovernance has to go through its own queue and land between day 7 and day 14. The Safe should schedule it right after proposing the handover, with a timelock delay of at most 14 days.

10.4Stage 4: the voting token

VORA is the project's planned voting token; GRET itself never votes. For the Governor to count votes, VORA must be built on OpenZeppelin's ERC20Votes, which keeps a history of each holder's voting power.

  • Voting power must be activated. Holding VORA is not enough: a holder must delegate its votes, to itself or to someone it trusts. Until then, its tokens do not count.
  • Votes are counted at a fixed moment. Each proposal takes a snapshot of voting power when voting opens. Buying, borrowing or receiving VORA afterwards changes nothing, so a flash loan cannot swing a vote.
  • Delegates. Holders who do not follow every proposal can delegate to a representative and take the votes back at any time.

10.5A proposal from start to finish

  1. Propose. A holder with enough voting power (the proposal threshold) submits a list of exact calls, for example "call vault.announceComponent(X)", with a description. The calls cannot be changed afterwards.
  2. Wait (Pending). The voting delay gives holders time to read the proposal and delegate. Voting power is fixed when it ends. The proposer can still cancel during this phase.
  3. Vote (Active). During the voting period, each holder votes For, Against or Abstain with the power it had at the snapshot.
  4. Count. The proposal succeeds if For plus Abstain reach the quorum (a share of all VORA at the snapshot) and For is strictly larger than Against. Otherwise it is defeated.
  5. Queue. A successful proposal is sent to the timelock, where its calls wait the timelock's delay in public.
  6. Execute. After the delay, the calls run from the timelock, in order and all together, exactly as voted.

10.6Settings to choose

These numbers are set when the Governor is deployed and can later be changed only by a vote. The values below are common starting points, not decisions.

SettingExampleWhat it protects against
Voting delay1 daySurprise votes: time to read a proposal and delegate before power is fixed
Voting period5 to 7 daysVotes that pass before most holders notice

Quorum4% to 10% of VORAA small group deciding while everyone else is absent

The last two are optional OpenZeppelin extensions (GovernorPreventLateQuorum and GovernorProposalGuardian).

10.7Who holds what at stage 4

  • The vault's governance is the timelock.
  • The Governor is the timelock's proposer and canceller. The executor can be the Governor or open to anyone.
  • The Safe stops being a proposer. OpenZeppelin warns that any extra proposer could act as the timelock without a vote. The Safe can stay as proposal guardian, with power only to cancel.
  • The timelock has no admin: only a vote can change its roles.

10.8Example: adding a token by vote

DayWhat happens
0A VORA holder proposes one call: vault.announceComponent(X). Everyone can read it on the chain and on Tally.

A proposal can carry several calls that run together. Syncing an issuer distribution safely becomes one vote with three calls, setMintPaused(true), sync(token) and setMintPaused(false), which run in the same transaction, so nobody can mint in between even if anyone may execute. The proposal itself is public for days, during the vote and the timelock, so someone who sees it can mint before and share in the sync; small, frequent syncs keep that gain small.

10.10Tools

  • Tally works with OpenZeppelin's Governor: it lists proposals, decodes their calls, and lets holders delegate, vote, queue and execute from the browser.
  • Safe provides the multisig interface for stages 2 and 3, including batches of calls.
  • Snapshot offers free off-chain polls. They are useful to sound out opinion but do not bind the vault unless a separate tool connects them to a Safe; they are not needed here.

Chapter 11

Safety

No contract is risk-free. This chapter separates what the code enforces from what depends on people and issuers, and lists every risk that was accepted and why.

11.1What the code guarantees

  • Nobody issues GRET without depositing the basket: governance has no mint.
  • Reserves leave only when GRET is burned or another component is delivered in exchange.
  • The code never changes: no upgrades.
  • A new rebalancer, token, governance, write-off or fee raise waits 7 days, visible on chain, and expires 7 days after that.
  • Fees never exceed 5% each.
  • Governance can never take what was written off.
  • Redemption can never be paused, and whoever minted can always leave with every token that still works.
  • The limits you sign apply only to the basket you saw: if a token is added or removed in between, your transaction reverts.
  • Every swap passes the price guard; with the Chainlink guard, swaps lose at most 2% each and 2% of the vault per week.
  • In a liquidation, every holder can exit token by token, even if some token fails.

11.2What it does not

  • The price of swaps, fully. A malicious rebalancer can still take up to the guard's weekly limit until it is revoked, or more if a token trades below the asset its feed prices, and a captured governance can appoint a guard that accepts everything; the 7-day notice is the defense against that. Six tokens have no price feed and cannot be swapped.
  • An exit for every holder during the notice. Only wallets that minted can redeem. The others have to mint first, which governance can block by pausing minting; otherwise they depend on selling their GRET or on a liquidation, which only governance can start.
  • The issuers. All 11 tokens are upgradeable; their issuers can freeze, charge or change them.
  • Late minters. Whoever mints after a distribution arrives or a written-off token recovers, and before governance syncs it, shares in it without having paid. Pausing minting only stops those who arrive after the pause; a small mint fee makes it unprofitable for distributions, and keeping minting paused covers a recovery.
  • The absence of bugs. The contract has had internal reviews, not an independent audit.
  • Everything else that was weighed and accepted: section 11.4.

11.3The pilot

In the pilot, governance is the pilot wallet. Whoever obtains its key can start a liquidation, withdraw surpluses and, after a 7-day wait visible on chain, appoint themselves rebalancer and exchange the reserves for anything. If the key is lost, nobody can liquidate, sync or change the basket. Before third-party funds, governance should move to a Safe and the contract should be audited.

Chapter 12

Questions and answers

Short answers to the questions people ask first, with a pointer to where the full explanation is.

12.1Can governance take my tokens?

Not directly. It cannot withdraw reserves, mint GRET or touch liquidation rights. Its most dangerous power is indirect: appointing a rebalancer, and a price guard that accepts any price. That takes a 7-day public notice, during which anyone who minted can redeem, skipping any token that was made to fail (chapters 3 and 7). With the Chainlink guard in place, even a stolen rebalancer key can lose at most 2% of the vault per week.

12.2I bought GRET on an exchange. Why can't I redeem it?

Normal redemption is only for wallets that have minted at least once. Mint any amount once from the same wallet and you can redeem everything you hold, including what you bought. While governance has minting paused you cannot do that first mint, so you have to wait or sell. In a liquidation, every holder can exit without this condition (sections 3.1 and 8.2).

12.3What happens if an issuer freezes the vault?

Minting stops and plain redemptions revert, because that token cannot move. Holders who minted can leave at once with redeemSkipping, giving up their part of the frozen token; those who never minted cannot qualify until the token is written off, because their first mint would have to deposit it. Governance can write it off after 7 days so the vault works normally again, and sync returns it to the holders if the freeze ends (sections 3.4 and 6.5).

12.4What if an issuer takes tokens from the vault or charges a fee?

If the vault holds less than it recorded, the loss is recognized and shared by every holder in proportion. A token that charges a fee on transfers makes mints revert for it, so it should not be in the basket (section 5.3).

12.5Can the basket change without me knowing?

A new token always waits 7 days in public before it can join, and so does a new rebalancer. Once a rebalancer is in place, though, its swaps take effect at once: the proportions can change from one block to the next. What never changes without notice is who is allowed to swap (sections 6.3 and 7.2).

12.6Why is there a 1 GRET floor?

Without it, someone holding almost everything could redeem down to a few wei, and the rounding leftovers would distort the basket so much that minting became unusable. The cost is that the last bit of the last large holder may need a liquidation, or the GRET gathered in one wallet, to come out (section 3.3).

12.7Does GRET have a price?

Not inside the contract, which only counts tokens. GRET is worth whatever stands behind it, valued at the market prices of the basket's tokens. If it trades on an exchange, its market price can drift from that value, which is what makes minting and redeeming worthwhile for arbitrageurs.

12.8Can the contract be upgraded?

No. Its code is fixed for good. Everything that can change (the basket, fees, the rebalancer, governance) changes within rules the code already contains. Anything beyond that requires a new vault, and holders would move voluntarily by redeeming from the old one and minting in the new one.

12.9Do I earn anything by holding GRET?

GRET has no yield of its own. Issuer distributions that arrive as balance, such as the xStocks multiplier increases, are synced into the reserves and belong to all holders. Value otherwise moves with the prices of the basket's tokens.

12.10Can I lose money?

Yes. The basket's tokens can lose value; an issuer can freeze, seize or change its token; a rebalancer can trade at a bad price; and the contract can have bugs, since it has had internal reviews but no independent audit yet (chapter 11).

12.11What if the pilot wallet's key is lost or stolen?

Lost: nobody can change the basket, sync, rescue or start a liquidation, but holders can still mint and redeem. Stolen: the thief can start a liquidation, withdraw surpluses and, after a public 7-day notice, appoint a rebalancer and swap the reserves away. Holders who minted can leave during that notice (section 11.3).

TermMeaning
